### Explicit mode per schedule

Every schedule requires an explicit mode. Agents cannot silently choose or
inherit a mode; one conversation may have schedules with different modes.

| Mode | Required behavior |
| --- | --- |
| This session only | Cancel future wakes when Codex exits or its pane/tab is destroyed. Byobu detach and terminal disconnection do not count as closure. A confirmed process crash does; uncertain connectivity does not. |
| Re-open session live | Resume the exact saved conversation, creating a Byobu tab and starting Codex when needed, using its most recently verified harness and working directory. |
| Background | Use the exact conversation in its live TUI when available; otherwise continue it through the app server without opening a visible tab. |

The continuing modes recover scheduling after monitor restart and machine reboot.
Session-only schedules stay cancelled once recipient process loss is confirmed.
Missing or unsupported restoration settings hold delivery rather than substituting
another harness, directory, or conversation. Latest verified settings follow the
conversation, rather than freezing settings at registration.

### Durable recurrence and explicit registration

Codex Wake alone owns recurrence and computes each occurrence. Checking agents
register schedules and perform checks; future occurrences do not depend on model
re-arming. This resolves the Wake-versus-codex-bots schedule-authority question.
The codex-bots integration interface remains to be discovered before its own work.

Scheduling agent and operator explicitly agree on:

- mode and exact conversation;
- cadence, with named timezone and skipped/repeated-time behavior when clock-based;
- end date, scheduled-occurrence count, or until-cancelled condition;
- missed-run policy: skip, latest-only, or bounded catch-up, plus maximum lateness;
- overlap policy: skip, latest-only coalescing, or bounded queue;
- occurrence delivery window.

Twice daily does not inherently mean fixed clock times or twelve elapsed hours.
Elapsed-time cadence retains elapsed-time semantics. No perpetual schedule or
other missing selection is silently inferred. A count means scheduled occurrences,
including skipped/expired ones; failures do not extend it. Successful-check counts
are a separate completion condition, outside this initial schedule-count contract.

The registration receipt exposes the agreed fields and monitor readiness before
claiming active scheduling. Saved schedules without a compatible working monitor
are retained but inactive. Preserve missed occurrences and original due times.

### Execution ownership and recovery

Queue due instructions while the recipient is busy, without interrupting its
current turn, subject to the occurrence deadline and explicit overlap policy.
Downtime misses and overlap are separate policy choices. Never execute concurrent
checks in the same conversation, including checks from different schedules.

Uncertain foreground/background ownership holds delivery and is reported; verify
ownership before starting either route. When the operator opens a conversation
running a background check, reconnect to that execution where supported, otherwise
wait until it finishes before transferring control. Preserve the check and single
execution owner; opening a tab does not authorize interruption or a second copy.

Failed harness startup, missing directories and unresolved ownership retain the
schedule and put affected checks into the operator attention mechanism with the
reason and original deadline. Avoid repeated launch attempts until resolution.
Other schedules continue independently. Delivery uncertainty requires evidence
reconciliation, never blind replay. Registration cannot claim an unsupported
runtime route is available.

### Changes, cancellation and authority

Explicit edits create schedule revisions for future checks while preserving past
occurrence identities and history, including started/missed checks. Never reset
uncertain delivery or silently replay a historical occurrence.

Cancellation stops future occurrences and queued deliveries. Already running
checks may finish; stopping one needs a separate explicit instruction. Session-only
closure is an additional cancellation condition. Other modes follow their declared
end condition.

A wake resumes only the agreed task under existing permissions and resource limits.
Restoration does not expand task scope, permissions or allowance. Checks report
requirements for additional authority instead of assuming it.

### Bounded implementation handoff

After final design confirmation, reconcile existing lifetime/startup policies and
terminal/app-server ownership mechanisms against this contract before creating
implementation tickets. Existing shipped defaults are not equivalent to explicit
per-schedule mode selection. Keep implementation packets small, reuse existing
capabilities, and identify missing runtime qualifications explicitly. No production
registration, dispatch, terminal manipulation, deployment or provider action is
authorized by this design interview.
